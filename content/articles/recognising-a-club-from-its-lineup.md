A starting XI is more than eleven names. The formation, age profile, player nationalities and distribution of roles can create a recognisable picture of a club. Squad puzzles ask you to notice that picture and connect it to a team.

The trick is to avoid treating every clue as an isolated fact. One familiar player may suggest several clubs, but the formation and the rest of the lineup can make one answer much more likely.

## Read the formation before the names

Start by looking at how the players are arranged. A formation can point to a coach’s preferred structure or to the types of players available in the squad. It is a clue rather than proof, but it can guide which teams you consider first.

Notice the balance across the pitch. Is the team built around wide attackers, several central midfielders or wing-backs? Those patterns can help you test whether your first club guess fits the whole lineup.

## Combine nationality, age and role

A nationality by itself rarely identifies a team, and an age by itself is even less precise. But a cluster of international backgrounds, an experienced central player and younger wide players can create a profile that feels familiar to followers of a particular club.

Use more than one clue before settling on an answer. If the club you are thinking of explains one player but not the rest of the shape, keep looking. Strong solutions account for the complete lineup rather than a single standout detail.

## Think like a squad builder

Ask how the players might fit together. Who provides width? Which player could organise the defence? Are there clues to a high press or a team that protects the centre? Thinking about roles turns a set of attributes into an image of how the side might play.
