A player clue is most useful when it is considered alongside the rest of the board. A position may describe a huge group of footballers, but combine it with a club, league and nationality and the candidate list can become much smaller.

Footle gives feedback after each guess so that you can adjust your thinking. You do not need to recognise the answer immediately: the game is designed to let you learn something from every attempt and gradually build a clearer picture of the mystery player.

## Separate exact matches from useful near matches

An exact match tells you that a category lines up with the target. A close result means the answer is related but not identical, such as a nearby age or another club in the same league. A mismatch tells you that the category should not be treated as an anchor for the next guess.

Do not interpret a close result as a hidden confirmation. It is a direction for further thought, not proof. Keep the exact and close clues separate so that you do not build a shortlist on assumptions the board has not confirmed.

## Choose an informative next guess

After each turn, ask what you still do not know. If the league appears close but the club is wrong, test another club from that competition. If the nationality points to a continent but not a country, choose a player who helps you compare the other categories too.

A guess is valuable when its feedback can rule in or rule out several possibilities. This is why a deliberate player from a plausible position and league is often better than a famous name chosen without a reason.

## Use football knowledge without overcommitting

Remember that squads change and player roles can be listed differently across data sources. A footballer may be associated with multiple positions, and a transfer can make a player’s club history complicated. Use the board’s feedback as the game’s source of truth for the current puzzle.

When you are unsure, preserve more than one possibility. A good shortlist keeps the likely players open until the clues distinguish them. That patience makes the final answer feel earned rather than lucky.
