Picking eleven great footballers does not automatically produce a great team. A side needs players who cover different spaces, share defensive responsibility and offer useful options when it attacks. Balance is what turns a list of talented individuals into a team with a clear shape.

That is the central decision in a World Cup draft game such as 8-0. The formation sets the starting structure, but the player roles determine how the team might cope with different phases of a match.

## Start with the shape

A formation is a way to organise the available players, not a guarantee that the team will behave in one fixed pattern. A back four may be supported by a single holding midfielder or two central players. A front three may contain touchline wingers, inside forwards or a mix of both.

Before selecting players, ask what the formation needs. Consider who will protect the defence, who can progress the ball and whether the forwards can create chances in more than one way.

## Balance strengths and responsibilities

A team full of attacking players may look exciting, but it still needs people to recover possession and defend space. Likewise, a cautious selection may struggle to create enough chances. Aim for complementary skills: pace alongside passing, creativity alongside ball-winning and width alongside central options.

Think about how the team responds when possession is lost. If the full-backs move forward, someone may need to cover behind them. If several players prefer to receive in the same area, the side could become crowded and easier to defend against.

## Adapt the draft to the era

A tournament pool spanning different decades can include players shaped by different tactical trends. Compare footballers in the context of their own era, and focus on the role they can fill in your chosen side rather than judging every selection by the same modern template.
