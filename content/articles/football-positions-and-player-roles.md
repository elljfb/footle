A team sheet groups players into positions, but a position is only the starting point for understanding a footballer’s role. Two midfielders might stand close together before kick-off and then have completely different responsibilities once the match begins.

Learning the broad positional groups is useful whether you are watching a match, discussing tactics or playing a football quiz. It gives you a framework for understanding where a player operates and what the team may ask them to do.

## The goalkeeper and defensive line

The goalkeeper protects the goal and is often the first player to start a team’s possession. Modern goalkeepers may be asked to pass under pressure and position themselves outside the penalty area when their team defends high up the pitch.

Defenders protect central areas and wide channels. Centre-backs often deal with forwards and defend the space in front of goal, while full-backs and wing-backs may provide width, support attacks and recover quickly when possession changes.

## Midfielders connect the team

Midfielders link defence and attack, but their responsibilities vary. A holding midfielder may protect the defenders and help circulate possession. A central midfielder can contribute in several phases, while an attacking midfielder looks for space closer to the opposition goal.

Wide midfielders and wingers also have different starting positions and jobs depending on the system. Some stay near the touchline to stretch the pitch; others move inside to combine, create chances or shoot.

## Forwards and flexible roles

A centre-forward may lead the line, hold up the ball, attack crosses or press the opposing defenders. A second striker or inside forward can operate around them, looking for space between the lines or in the channels.

Footballers are not always limited to one label. Formations change during matches, and players often move between roles. Watching how they behave in and out of possession usually tells you more than the position printed next to their name.
